Mali

Mali

Territorial integrity, Tuareg separatism and global governance

1. Context

Tuareg territory stretches across modern borders in the Sahel and Sahara.

Azawad is the northeast area claimed by Tuareg separatists.

Mali is a poor, landlocked country. The north is desert and semi-desert dominated by Tuareg groups, while the south contains the capital Bamako and most economic activity.
  • Main exports: gold, cotton and agricultural products.
  • Dependency: Mali relies heavily on foreign aid and migrant remittances.
  • Internal contrast: the north is remote and marginalised; the Bamako-based government is around 1000 km to the south.
2. Threats to Sovereignty and Territorial Integrity
  • Separatism: Tuareg groups declared independence for Azawad in northeast Mali.
  • Colonial legacy: European colonial boundaries divided Tuareg lands more than 100 years ago with little regard for tribal territories.
  • Marginalisation: the north has been neglected by the national government, encouraging claims for autonomy and fairer representation.
  • 2012 coup context: dissatisfaction with ineffective governance of the north contributed to political instability.
Terrorism and Porous Borders
  • Insurgent groups linked to Al-Qaeda, Islamic State and other ethnic or political affiliations expanded.
  • Attacks targeted Malian, regional, UN and French peacekeeping forces.
  • Arms, people and contraband moved across remote borders with Niger and Burkina Faso.
  • Schools and health facilities were attacked, increasing vulnerability to forced labour, sex exploitation and recruitment of children.
3. Global Governance Strategies
Actor Strategy
MINUSMA UN stabilisation mission with 12,000 military personnel, 1700 police and US$1.14 billion 2019/20 budget; supports peace, elections, rule of law and civilian protection.
UN agencies UNHCR, OCHA, WFP, UNDP and UNICEF support refugees, food security, remote access, SDGs, hygiene, health, education and child protection.
Regional organisations G5 Sahel, ECOWAS and the African Union support security and mediation.
Other states and NGOs French troops support security; British forces train Sahel counter-terrorism forces; NGOs such as Care, Oxfam, MSF and Solidarites International provide aid.
4. Opportunities and Remaining Challenges

Stability

Restoring sovereign control, rule of law, civilian protection, fairer representation and democratic elections.

Growth

Strengthening mining governance, diversifying industry and improving access to global supply chains.

Development

Improving health, education, food security, living conditions and small-holder farming resilience.

Injustice

Gender inequality, child marriage, forced labour, displacement, weak policing and poor access to education remain major issues.

Mali is your clearest example for territorial integrity: the recognised state is trying to prevent Azawad breaking away while also restoring authority in the north.
