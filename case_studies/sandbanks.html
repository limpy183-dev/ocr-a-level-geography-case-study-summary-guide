Coastal Management at Sandbanks, Dorset

Hold the Line — Beach Recharge & Hard Engineering

1. Location and Geography
  • Location: Sandbanks is a peninsula located in Poole Bay, Dorset (Southern England).
  • Geography: It separates Poole Harbour (to the West) from Poole Bay.
  • Scale: The wider bay stretches 16 km from Sandbanks to Hengistbury Head in the east.
2. Why is Management Needed? (Reasons for Protection)

The management strategy is driven by the high economic and social value of the land, as well as physical factors.

A. Economic Value
Property Prices: Sandbanks has some of the highest property values in the world. It is ranked the 4th most expensive area in the world per square metre.
  • Luxury apartments cost over £2 million.
  • Detached houses can cost over £10 million.
  • Often referred to as a "Tiny millionaires' playground" (Daily Mail).
  • Commercial Business: High-value commercial properties include the Sandbanks Hotel and the Haven Hotel. These provide significant employment and generate local income.
B. Tourism and Recreation
  • The Beach: A major tourist attraction with a Blue Flag award for water quality, making it safe for family swimming.
  • Poole Harbour: The peninsula provides protection and shelter from waves for the harbour. This makes it a popular and safe area for water sports (wind-surfing, sailing, water-skiing).
  • Yacht Clubs: The harbour is home to numerous marinas and clubs, such as Salterns Marina and Parkstone Yacht Club.
C. Physical Factors
  • Climate Change: The area is vulnerable to rising sea levels.
  • Erosion: The beach is subject to erosion from SW storm waves. Without the beach, the peninsula and the properties on it would be lost.
  • Geology: The area faces issues with local land down-warping (sinking).
3. Management Strategies Implemented
The responsibility for management lies with the Poole Harbour Commissioners, Poole Borough Council, and the Environment Agency. The overall strategy within the Shoreline Management Plan (SMP) is to "Hold the Line" (maintain the coastline in its present position).
A. Hard Engineering
Rock Groynes: Constructed to minimize longshore drift. This restricts the movement of sediment along the coast, keeping the beach in place to absorb wave energy.
B. Soft Engineering (Beach Recharge)

This is used to conserve the beaches and works with natural processes.

  • Rainbowing: Sand dredged from offshore is sprayed onto the beach. Cost: approximately £20 per m³.
  • Offshore Sediment Dumping: A recent trial involved dumping sediment dredged from the harbour just offshore. Natural currents then transport this sand onshore to build up the beach. Cost: much cheaper, at only £3 per m³.
Total Volume: In total, over 3.5 million m³ of sediment have been added to Poole Bay beaches.
4. Impacts on the Coastal System
  • Sediment Budget: The strategies intentionally alter the sediment budget. By adding 3.5 million m³ of sediment, the budget moves into a surplus (positive budget).
  • Processes: Rock groynes interrupt the natural flow of Longshore Drift, trapping sediment.
  • Landforms: The beach profile is changed — it becomes wider and higher.
5. Success of the Strategy
Objective Met: The aim of the Shoreline Management Plan is to 'hold the line'.

Evaluation: The strategies have been highly effective. The combination of rock groynes and beach recharge has not only maintained the coastline but has resulted in the width of the beach increasing.

Outcome: The coastline is actually advancing slightly (extending seawards), proving the management is working successfully with nature.
