Flooding & Disease in Bangladesh

Hazard Chain — From Flood to Disease Outbreaks

1. Geographical Context & Vulnerability
Bangladesh is known as a "Land of Rivers," making it exceptionally prone to flooding due to a combination of physical and human factors.
Physical Factors
  • Deltaic Landscape: Formed by the confluence of 3 major rivers: the Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Meghna. These rivers carry massive volumes of water and sediment from the Himalayas.
  • Subsidence: The region is slowly sinking due to plate tectonics, increasing flood vulnerability.
  • Climate Change: Increases the frequency/intensity of rainfall and glacial melt.
Human Factors
  • Population Density: Extremely high, often exceeding 1,000 people per square kilometre.
  • Agriculture: Over 60% of the population is rural, relying on fertile soil deposited by floods, which places huge numbers of people directly in the path of floodwaters.
2. The Hazard Chain: From Flood to Disease
Natural hazards trigger a sequence of events leading to disease outbreaks.
Stage 1Heavy Rainfall/Melt: Causes rivers to burst their banks.
Stage 2Flooding: Low-lying land is inundated.
Stage 3Contamination: Sewage mixes with floodwater, contaminating drinking sources.
Stage 4Outbreak: Pathogens spread, causing waterborne diseases.
Stage 5Illness/Death: Cholera, dysentery, and typhoid affect the population.
3. The Event: The 2020 Floods
The 2020 floods were a "devastating event" complicated by the Covid-19 crisis, putting immense strain on the health system.
Key Impacts (Social & Economic)

  • People Affected: 3.3 million people were affected by the floods.
  • Housing: 750,000 homes were waterlogged, displacing families.
  • Agriculture: 110,000 hectares of crops were destroyed, threatening food security and livelihoods.
  • Education: 1,900 schools were damaged or destroyed.
Health Impacts (Disease)
  • 4,500 people fell ill specifically from contaminated water.
  • This led to outbreaks of diarrhoea, typhoid, and hepatitis.
4. Management Strategies (Responses)

Bangladesh has implemented successful strategies to break the hazard chain and manage health risks during floods.

  • Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS): Widespread distribution and teaching regarding simple, effective ORS to treat dehydration caused by diarrhoeal diseases.
  • Hygiene Promotion: Government and NGO programs encouraging handwashing and the use of sanitary latrines.
  • Water Purification: Increased access to and knowledge of water purification tablets during and after floods.
  • Female Education: Improvements in female education have empowered mothers to provide better nutrition and care to children.
5. Evaluation: Success and Trends
Despite the severity of flood hazards, Bangladesh has made "world-class" progress in managing the associated health risks.

Summary: While climate change and population density continue to increase flood risks (with high-magnitude floods occurring frequently since 1972), the health impacts (mortality) are being successfully mitigated through education, sanitation, and medical interventions.
