Malaria in Ethiopia

Causes, Impacts, and Management Strategies

1. Incidence & Patterns
75%Land Area Endemic
70kDeaths/Year
2/3Pop at Risk
  • High Risk: Western Lowlands (Tigray, Amhara, Gambella).
  • Parasites: Caused by P. falciparum (60%) and P. vivax (40%).
  • Seasonal: Transmission peaks after rainy season (June–Nov).
2. Causes of Malaria
Environmental
  • Altitude: Mosquitoes thrive in warm, humid lowlands. Highlands are too cold for the parasite to develop.
  • Breeding Sites: Stagnant surface water.
Human
  • Migration: Harvest migration (June-Sept) moves people from highlands to lowlands during peak transmission.
  • Harvesting: Often continues after sunset when mosquitoes are active; workers sleep in fields.
  • Irrigation: Projects (Awash Valley) and rice cultivation create more breeding sites.
  • Urbanisation: Garbage dumps and discarded containers collect water.
  • Drug Resistance: Parasites becoming resistant to drugs.
3. Socio-Economic Impacts
Economic Cost: Lost production in Sub-Saharan Africa is ~$12 billion/year. In Ethiopia, malaria absorbs 40% of national health expenditure.
  • Poverty Cycle: Debilitating effects cause absenteeism, slowing growth.
  • Resources: Western lowlands are resource-rich but under-developed due to malaria.
  • Over-population: Malaria-free highlands have high pop density -> land degradation -> famine risk.
4. Strategies to Control & Treat
Treatment & Prevention
  • Drugs: Chloroquine (issues with resistance) and Mefloquine (serious psychological side effects). ACTs (Artemisinin-based therapies) now standard.
  • Vector Control: Insecticide-treated nets (ITNs) and Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS). >70% households protected (2015).
  • Campaigns: "Roll Back Malaria" (RBM) partnership. National Malaria Strategic Plan (NMSP).
Future Goals
  • Elimination 2030: Target aligned with WHO Global Technical Strategy.
  • Success: On track to reduce incidence by 40% by 2020.
  • Challenges: Insecticide resistance and migrant populations.
