Tectonic Context:
  • Located where 4 plate boundaries meet.
  • Subduction zone: The Pacific Plate is subducting underneath the North American Plate at a rate of 8 cm per year.
  • Seismic History: Japan experiences ~400 quakes a day. Since 2000, 23 high-magnitude quakes have killed over 20,000 people.
2. The Event Profile
9.0Magnitude
6 minDuration
30 kmShallow Focus
  • Date: March 11th, 2011 (2:46 PM).
  • Rank: 4th largest earthquake in the world since 1900.
  • Epicentre: 70 km east of the Oshika Peninsula.
  • Type: Undersea mega-thrust earthquake.
3. Geophysical Effects (The Power of the Quake)
  • Tectonic Movement: The seabed rose by 7 m and moved west by 40–50 m. The Pacific plate slipped westwards by between 20 and 40 m.
  • Land Movement: Honshu island moved 2.4 m east. A 400 km stretch of coastline dropped vertically by 0.6 m (subsidence).
  • Planetary Impact: The Earth shifted on its axis by 10–25 cm, shortening the day by 1.8 microseconds.
  • Aftershocks: Over 800 earthquakes of magnitude 4.5 or more recorded following the main event.
4. The Tsunami
40 mMax Wave Height
10 kmInland Reach
  • Wave Height: Reached up to 40 m in places.
  • Inland Reach: Travelled up to 10 km inland (e.g., in Sendai) and caused pollution up to 6 miles inland.
Global Reach:
  • Reached Antarctica (breaking off 125 km² of ice).
  • Reached California/Oregon, USA (waves up to 2.4 m causing $10m damage).
  • Midway Atoll: 1.5 m wave drowned 11,000 nesting birds.
5. Impacts
Social Impacts
15,894Deaths
6,152Injured
300,000Homeless
  • Deaths: 15,894 (90% from drowning). 2/3 of victims were over 60 years old.
  • Displacement: 130,927 displaced; 300,000 left homeless. 2,000 children orphaned or lost one parent.
  • Infrastructure: 457,000 buildings destroyed; 143,300 damaged. 4.4 million households without electricity. 300 hospitals damaged (11 destroyed).
  • Health: Burial traditions were waived (mass graves used) to prevent disease.
Economic Impacts
  • Cost: Estimated between US $181–235 billion (the most expensive natural disaster in history).
  • Industry:
    • 4 ports in the North East were destroyed.
    • 10% of fishing ports damaged.
    • Sony factories closed, causing a shortage of microchips in the UK.
    • 2 oil refineries set on fire.
  • Energy: Japan's nuclear production was halted.
Political & Nuclear Impacts (Fukushima)
Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Plant:
  • The 5.7 m sea wall was overwhelmed by the 15 m tsunami.
  • 3 of 6 reactors stopped working (meltdown).
  • 80,000 people within a 12-mile radius were evacuated.
  • Radioactivity in the local area exceeded the legal limit by 4,000 times.
  • Global Reaction: Anti-nuclear protests occurred in Japan and Germany.
6. Preparation and Mitigation
  • Early Warning: Japan has the world's densest seismometer network. Tokyo received a 90-second warning before shaking started.
  • Education: Annual drills on September 1st. Schools in Kamaishi successfully evacuated 3,000 children to high ground.
  • Engineering: Billions spent on earthquake-proofing buildings.
Failures: Prediction systems estimated a max magnitude of 8.0; risk maps did not account for faults extending 500 km.
7. Responses
Short-term (Immediate)
  • Survey: Aircraft in the air within 30 minutes to survey damage.
  • Personnel: 100,000 people took part in disaster relief; 1,600 volunteers/military in rescue operations.
  • Shelter: Army built temporary shelters; Shelterbox (NGO) sent 1,500 relief boxes.
  • Aid: Japanese Red Cross received over $1 billion in donations and distributed 30,000 emergency relief kits.
Long-term (Recovery)
  • Reconstruction Agency: Established in Feb 2012 with $25 billion given to local governments for recovery.
  • Housing: Scheme to build "compact cities" on higher ground. However, 330,000 people were still in temporary housing years later.
  • Cleanup: 25 million tonnes of debris generated.
  • Economy: The Bank of Japan injected money into the economy to bring stability.
