Cancer in the UK

Social, Economic & Cultural Causes and Impacts

1. Causes
Environmental Risk Factors: Explain at least two-thirds of cancer cases in most ACs.
  • Cultural/Lifestyle:
    • Sunbeds/Tanning: Cultural preference for tanning increases skin cancer risk (3% year-on-year rise).
    • Diet: Preference for meat, dairy, and fast food correlated with bowel cancer.
    • Alcohol: Increases risks of oral/liver cancer.
    • Smoking: Remains biggest single cause (1/5 of all cases).
  • Socio-Economic: Positive association between wealth and some cancers (breast/prostate), but deprivation increases likelihood of smoking/obesity/alcohol.
  • Regional: Glasgow has the highest rate. In England, rates are 3x higher in poorest areas (e.g., Liverpool/Manchester).

  • Economic Loss: Premature deaths remove productive workers (~35,000 working age deaths/year).
  • Survivors: Many do not return to work or reduce hours due to fatigue/mental health.
  • Care: Over 1 million people care for someone with cancer, impacting their own productivity.
  • Deprivation Gap: Survival rates are lower in deprived areas due to later diagnosis and pre-existing health status.
3. Management Strategies
Government Target: Save 5000 lives/year
Direct Strategies
  • Technology: Precise radiotherapy, endoscopy.
  • Screening: Mass screening for breast, cervical, and bowel cancer.
  • NHS: Reducing waiting times.
Indirect Strategies
  • Prevention: Health campaigns (smoking, diet).
  • Legislation: Controlling commmerical use of sunbeds (age limits, supervision). Met Office forecasts on UV intensities.
International Agencies
  • WHO: International Agency for Research on Cancer.
  • Charities: Cancer UK researches prevention/diagnosis/treatment.
