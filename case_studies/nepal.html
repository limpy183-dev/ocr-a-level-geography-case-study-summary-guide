The Gorkha Earthquake, Nepal (2015)

LIDC — Magnitude 7.8 Collision Zone Earthquake

1. The Event Profile
7.8Magnitude
15 kmShallow Focus
April 25thDate (2015)
  • Epicentre: 90km NE of Kathmandu (the capital).
  • Aftershocks: There were 51 aftershocks equivalent to magnitude 5, and 5 aftershocks above magnitude 6 (including a 7.6 magnitude shock in May).
2. Tectonic Causes & Context
  • Plate Margin: Situated in a Collision Zone where the Indo-European plate meets the Eurasian plate.
  • Process: The plates grind against each other, building pressure which is eventually released as earthquakes.
  • Geological Risk: The seismic risk is increased by the geological structure. The sediment is very soft, which exacerbates seismic waves, causing stronger shaking and creating a risk of liquefaction (where soil acts like a liquid due to water presence).
Frequency: On average, Nepal experiences two 7-8 magnitude quakes every 40 years and one 8 magnitude quake every 80 years.
3. Primary Impacts (Direct Effects)
Social Impacts

22,000People Injured
2.8MDisplaced
  • Deaths: Between 8,632 and 8,800 people died in Nepal. 160 deaths occurred in other countries (mainly India).
  • Injuries: Approximately 19,000 to 22,000 people injured.
  • Displacement: Over 2.8 million people were displaced in the Kathmandu valley alone.
  • Homelessness: 3 million people were made homeless; hundreds of thousands of homes were flattened.
  • Housing: 473,000 houses were destroyed or badly damaged.
  • Immediate Needs: 1.4 million people required support for water, food, and shelter immediately after the quake.
Economic Impacts
  • Cost: The USGS estimated the economic cost at $10 billion (other estimates cite US $5 billion).
  • GDP Impact: Economic losses were estimated to be between 9% to 50% of Nepal's GDP.
  • Infrastructure: 50% of shops were destroyed, affecting food supplies and livelihoods. 7,000 schools were destroyed.
  • Cultural: World Heritage sites were damaged, including Bhaktapur and the Dharahara Tower (one of the world's oldest Buddhist buildings).
4. Secondary Impacts (Indirect Effects)
Landslides & Avalanches
  • Mount Everest: An avalanche killed at least 17 to 20 climbers/people at Base Camp.
  • Langtang Region: An avalanche caused 250 people to go missing.
  • River Blockages: The Kali Gandaki River was blocked by a landslide, forcing evacuations due to flood risk.
  • Quake Lakes: Debris blocked steep valleys forming unstable dams. These pose a threat of bursting and causing flash floods downstream.
Other Secondary Effects
  • Tourism: Employment and income declined sharply. The climbing season on Everest was lost for the following year.
  • Agriculture: Rice seeds were ruined in rubble, causing food shortages and income loss. Harvests were reduced as the quake occurred during planting season (leading to food security issues in 2016).
  • Social Issues: An increase in human trafficking into brothels was reported. Single women struggled to receive aid as men tended to control distribution.
5. Vulnerability Factors
  • Inequality: The lower caste Tibeto-Burmese were most affected as they lived on high slopes prone to landslides, which were the hardest areas to reach with relief supplies.
  • Political: Nepal had recently become politically stable, but the earthquake increased regional tensions.
6. Management and Responses
Immediate Responses (Short-term)
  • International Aid: India and China provided over $1 billion in international aid.
  • UK Response: Sent over 100 search and rescue responders, medics, and experts, plus 3 Chinook helicopters.
  • Shelter: Half a million tents were provided. A 'Tent city' was established in Kathmandu.
  • Migration: 300,000 people migrated out of Kathmandu to seek support from friends and family.
  • Technology: Facebook launched a safety check feature; GIS "Crisis mapping" was used to coordinate response.
  • Search and Rescue: Helicopters rescued people from Everest and cut-off villages. Field hospitals were set up to relieve overwhelmed hospitals.
Long-term Responses (Recovery)
  • Financial Aid:
    • Asian Development Bank (ADB): Provided a $3 million grant for immediate relief and up to $200 million for rehabilitation.
    • UK Donations: £73 million donated total (£23m from government, £50m from the public).
  • Reconstruction:
    • Stricter building codes were introduced.
    • Over 7,000 schools were rebuilt.
    • Damaged homes were repaired and thousands re-housed.
  • Tourism Recovery: Repairs were made to Everest base camp and trekking routes. By August 2015, new routes were established and the mountain reopened to tourists.
  • Political/Trade: A blockade at the Indian border was cleared in late 2015, allowing the movement of fuel, medicine, and construction materials.
  • Environmental: Landslides were cleared, roads repaired, and lakes formed by landslides were drained to prevent flooding.
