Place Profiles

Toxteth, Liverpool

Urban Deprivation — Past & Present Characteristics

Part 1: Past Characteristics
1. Location and Physical Geography
  • Location: North West England, within the city of Liverpool.
  • Setting: Situated on the banks of the River Mersey.
  • Topography: Characterized by undulating land (wave-like/rolling land), which historically added complexity to its geographical profile.
  • Water Features: Presence of a stream and the River Mersey were crucial for its early function.
2. Timeline of Development (Shifting Flows)
11th Century (Saxon Era)Established as a small coastal village. Its position on the river made it a prime location for fishing.
13th Century (Royal Hunting Ground)King John acquired the land. It became a hunting park known as "Tock Steph Park" (Toxteth Park). Mix of woodland and open green space, ideal for hunting deer.
16th Century (Farming & Industry)The area opened up for farming. A stream was used for irrigation and later harnessed for water power, leading to small cottage industries.
18th Century (Trade & Wealth)Key stop-off point for the Slave Trade (transporting goods/people to America). Hub for importing Scandinavian timber. Global connections brought significant wealth.
19th Century (Rapid Urbanisation)Port expansion. Shift to manufacturing hub. Mass exodus of middle class to rural-urban fringe. In 30–40 years, the area transformed completely.
3. Evolution of the Built Environment

Phase 1: High-Class Residential

  • The Georgian Quarter: Fueled by wealth from slave trade and industry
  • Large Georgian and grand Victorian houses
  • Home to rich slave merchants and industrialists
  • Low-density "greenfield" environment

Phase 2: Working Class Industrial

  • Middle class left; spaces filled in
  • Extensive, cheap terraced housing
  • High-density living populated by dock/factory workers
  • Poor quality led to disease (e.g., cholera)
Key Factors Shaping Toxteth: Natural (River Mersey for fishing/trade), Global Connections (18th-century Slave Trade, Scandinavian timber), Socio-Economic (shift from wealthy merchants to working-class population changed the built environment from grand villas to cramped terraces).
Part 2: Present Characteristics
1. Location and Demographics
  • Location: Inner-city area in Liverpool, on the banks of the River Mersey.
  • Population Structure: High population density and diverse demographic makeup due to a long history of immigration.
Ethnicity: 78% White (contrast with Lympstone: 99% White). ~20% Ethnic Minorities: Black African Caribbean (7%), Asian (5%), Mixed (6%), Other (4%).
Migration History:
  • 19th Century: Arrival of African and Chinese sailors due to port trade.
  • Irish Migration: Large influx following the Potato Famines.
  • Post-WWII: Migration from the Commonwealth to help rebuild England.
2. Socio-Economic Characteristics

Toxteth is characterized by significant social and economic deprivation.

Deprivation: The area contains several sub-areas ranked among the most deprived in the country according to the Index of Multiple Deprivation (2015).

  • Home Ownership: Very low compared to the UK average.
  • Car Ownership: Significantly lower than the national average.
  • Impact: Many residents lack the qualifications to access new "knowledge economy" jobs.
  • Unemployment: Mass unemployment in the 1970s and 80s due to deindustrialisation led to the 1981 Toxteth Riots.
3. Cultural Characteristics
Religious Diversity: The built environment features places of worship for various faiths, including:
  • Greek Orthodox Church
  • Princes Road Synagogue (Jewish community)
  • Al Rahma Masjid (Mosque)
  • Christian churches (including Caribbean Christian communities)

Cultural Events: Cultural activities and festivals reflect this mix, contrasting with the monocultural (Christian) calendar of places like Lympstone.

4. Political Characteristics
  • Local Government: Toxteth lies within the Riverside Ward, one of 30 wards making up the Liverpool City Council.
  • Labour MP: The area has been a Labour stronghold since the 1980s.
  • Granby Four Streets CLT: A community-led regeneration project renovating derelict houses and focusing on affordable housing.
  • Past Influence: The Merseyside Development Corporation (MDC) was established by the central government in the 1980s to regenerate the docks.
5. Shifting Flows: Economy and Built Environment

Toxteth has undergone a massive transition from a maritime/industrial hub to a struggling inner-city area attempting regeneration.

A. Causes of Decline (1930s–1980s):
  • Containerisation: 19th-century docks were too small and shallow for modern container ships. The port moved north to Seaforth.
  • Trade Shift: UK trade shifted towards the EU, favouring ports in the South East; Liverpool was on the "wrong side" of the country.
  • Deindustrialisation: Factories closed or moved abroad, leading to mass unemployment for dockers and industrial workers.
B. Current Regeneration & The Future:
  • Rebranding: Attempting to shift the perception of Liverpool from "dirty/derelict" to a destination for tourism and culture.
  • European Capital of Culture (2008): Attracted significant investment.
  • Tate Liverpool: Converted old dock warehouses into an art gallery/museum to boost tourism.
  • Liverpool Science Park: Established to foster tertiary and quaternary industries (IT, finance, research).
  • Low Carbon Sector: Investment in large offshore wind farms in Liverpool Bay.
The Challenge: There is a mismatch between the new high-skilled jobs created (Knowledge Economy) and the skills possessed by the local Toxteth residents.
Question: Will the flow of investment actually help the residents of Toxteth, or just commuters from elsewhere? (Only 16% of students at Liverpool University are locals.)
