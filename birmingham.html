2. Structural Economic Change: The Decline

From the 1970s onwards, Birmingham experienced De-industrialisation, shifting from the Secondary sector (Manufacturing) to the Tertiary sector (Services).

Causes of Decline
  • Global Factors: The 1973 Arab-Israeli War led to an OPEC oil embargo and price hikes, damaging manufacturing costs.
  • Competition: Increased competition from foreign car manufacturers (e.g., Nissan, Toyota) and TNCs moving production to cheaper countries (Global Shift).
Decline of Traditional Industries:
  • Metal goods & vehicle employment: Dropped from 28.4% (1978) to 5.8% (2000).
  • Manufacturing Output (Midlands): Dropped from £15.3bn (1997) to £11.8bn (2010).
  • Longbridge Plant: Once employed 27,000 workers. By 2016, car production ceased entirely (MG moved to China).
  • Jewellery Quarter: Employment dropped from peak of 30,000 to 6,000 people across 400 businesses.

  • Urban Decay: Left derelict factories ("brownfield sites") and polluted canals.

3. Players Driving Change (Regeneration)
Key Players
  • Local Government (Birmingham City Council): Created the Birmingham Masterplan 2011-31.
  • National Government: Provided funding via the Single Regeneration Budget and City Challenge. Incentivized the NEC and Airport expansion.
  • The EU: Provided Social Investment Funds (for social housing/small enterprises) and Regional Development Funds.
  • TNCs & Private Investors: Banks (Lloyds, Deutsche Bank) and Retailers (Selfridges) invested in the CBD.
The Shift to Services (Tertiary Sector)
  • Finance: Employment in finance/business services rose from 7.3% (1978) to 21.4% (2000). Over 50,000 people now employed in financial services.
  • Key players: Lloyds Bank (founded in Birmingham, 1765), Deutsche Bank, PwC, and the Islamic Bank of Britain (HQ in Birmingham).
  • Jaguar Land Rover: Bucked the manufacturing decline trend. Employment at Solihull plant rose from 4,000 (2008) to over 10,000.

Birmingham Masterplan Goals (2011–2031):
  • Grow the City Core by 25%.
  • Create 1.5 million m² of new floorspace.
  • Create 50,000 new jobs contributing £2.1 billion to the economy annually.
  • Build 5,000+ new homes.
5. Socio-Cultural Impact (Migration & Diversity)

Structural change coincided with significant demographic shifts due to post-WWII migration.

Diversity:
  • Immigrants from Kashmir, Pakistan, and Bangladesh settled in inner-city areas (e.g., Small Heath).
  • "Balti Triangle": Home to over 50 curry houses; Birmingham is the self-proclaimed "Curry Capital of the UK".
Religion & Community:
  • Birmingham Central Mosque: Built in 1969 (2nd purpose-built mosque in the UK).
  • Eid Festival: In 2018, 140,000 people attended the celebration in Small Heath Park (Europe's largest).
  • Sport: Strong interest in cricket at Edgbaston driven by Asian immigrants and subsequent generations.
  • Jewellery Quarter: Employment has dropped to 6,000 people, but it remains a cultural heritage site.
