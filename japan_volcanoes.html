2. Case Study: Mount Ontake (2014)
Mount Ontake: A classic strato-volcano (3000m+) on Honshu island, 200km west of Tokyo. Often snow-covered and a place of spiritual pilgrimage.
  • History: Dormant for many centuries until a sequence of eruptions between Oct 1979 - April 1980.
  • Recent Activity: Further small phreatic eruptions in 1991 and 2007.
  • Tourism: Popular with climbers/trekkers; facilities included a lodge close to the summit.
The 2014 Eruption
  • Date: 27 September 2014, just before midday.
  • Type: Violent eruption without warning. No alerts were in place despite some increased earthquake activity.
  • Impacts:
    • 63 deaths: Mostly climbers and hikers on the slopes.
    • Large areas affected by ash fall, pyroclastic flows, volcanic bombs, and lahars.
Response & Lessons
  • Rescue: >200 survivors made it down. Search and rescue teams deployed; PM Shinzo Abe ordered military assistance.
  • Lesson: Even a country with sophisticated monitoring like Japan can be taken by surprise by the unpredictability of tectonic hazards.
