Global Migration Case Studies

Turkey, Germany, Rohingya Crisis & Ukraine Conflict

Case Study 1: Turkey — Migration Gateway
  • Role: Turkey is a major transit and host country for migrants and refugees due to its position between Asia, Europe, and the Middle East.
  • Syrian Refugees: Turkey hosts the largest refugee population in the world — over 3.6 million registered Syrian refugees.
Push Factors (Why People Leave)
  • Syrian Civil War (2011): Conflict, persecution, and destruction of homes and infrastructure.
  • Economic collapse: Livelihoods destroyed by bombing and sanctions.
  • Lack of services: Hospitals, schools, and water systems destroyed.
Pull Factors (Why Turkey)
  • Proximity: Turkey shares a 900 km border with Syria.
  • Open door policy: Initially, Turkey maintained an open border policy.
  • Cultural/Religious ties: Shared Muslim culture eased integration.
Impacts on Turkey
  • Economic: Strain on public services; estimated cost of hosting refugees exceeded $40 billion by 2020.
  • Social: Integration challenges; some tension between host communities and refugees.
  • Political: EU-Turkey deal (2016) — EU paid Turkey €6 billion to prevent migrants from crossing into Europe.
Case Study 2: Germany — Destination Country
  • Role: Germany was the primary destination for migrants during the European Migrant Crisis (2015+).
  • Policy: Chancellor Angela Merkel's "Willkommenskultur" (Welcome Culture) opened Germany's doors to asylum seekers.
In 2015 alone, Germany accepted over 890,000 asylum applications — more than any other European country.
Pull Factors
  • Economic Strength: Largest economy in Europe; strong job market.
  • Ageing Population: Germany needed younger workers to fill labour gaps and support its pension system.
  • Generous Welfare: Asylum seekers receive housing, language courses, and financial support.
  • Freedom and Rights: Strong democratic institutions and human rights protections.
Impacts on Germany

✅ Positive Impacts

  • Filled labour shortages in healthcare, manufacturing
  • Cultural enrichment and diversity
  • Younger demographic profile
  • Some refugees started successful businesses

⚠️ Negative Impacts

  • Strain on housing and services
  • Rise of far-right (AfD party) anti-migration sentiment
  • Integration challenges (language, culture)
  • Cost: Estimated €20 billion/year
Case Study 3: Rohingya Crisis (Myanmar → Bangladesh)
700,000+Refugees (2017)
1M+Total in Bangladesh
Background
  • Who: The Rohingya are a Muslim ethnic minority in Myanmar (Burma).
  • Persecution: Denied citizenship since 1982; described by the UN as "the world's most persecuted minority."
  • 2017 Crisis: Myanmar military launched a brutal crackdown (described as "ethnic cleansing" and "genocide" by the UN). Villages were burned, civilians were killed, and sexual violence was widespread.
The Exodus
  • Over 700,000 Rohingya fled into neighbouring Bangladesh in just a few months (August 2017 onwards).
  • Route: Crossed the Naf River and hills on foot, many arriving sick, injured, and traumatised.
  • Camp: Most settled in the Kutupalong refugee camp in Cox's Bazar — now the world's largest refugee settlement.
Impacts and Conditions
  • Overcrowding: Camps are dangerously overcrowded, with limited sanitation.
  • Health: Outbreaks of diphtheria, cholera, and measles; lack of medical facilities.
  • Environment: Massive deforestation around Cox's Bazar to build shelters, increasing vulnerability to landslides and cyclones during monsoon season.
  • Education: Limited access to formal education for Rohingya children.
  • Host Country Strain: Bangladesh, itself an LIDC, faces enormous pressure hosting over 1 million refugees.
International Response:
  • UN agencies (UNHCR, UNICEF, WFP) provide food, medical aid, and shelter materials.
  • International Criminal Court (ICC) opened investigations into Myanmar's actions.
  • Repatriation attempts have failed so far due to safety concerns in Myanmar.
Case Study 4: Ukraine Conflict (2022)
6M+Refugees Internationally
8M+Internally Displaced
Background
  • Event: Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.
  • Result: One of the fastest-growing displacement crises in recent history.
Patterns of Migration
  • Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs): Over 8 million Ukrainians displaced within the country.
  • International Refugees: Over 6 million fled to neighbouring countries.
  • Main Destinations: Poland (received the most), Romania, Hungary, Moldova, Slovakia, and across the EU.
  • Demographics: Predominantly women and children (men aged 18–60 were subject to martial law and could not leave).
Impacts

🇺🇦 Impacts on Ukraine

  • Massive infrastructure destruction
  • Brain drain (skilled workers leaving)
  • Heritage and cultural sites damaged
  • Agricultural disruption ("breadbasket of Europe")

🇵🇱🇪🇺 Impacts on Host Countries

  • Strain on housing, schools, and healthcare
  • Increased demand on social services
  • Some economic benefit (labour contribution)
  • EU Temporary Protection Directive activated
International & UK Response
  • EU Temporary Protection Directive: Activated for the first time, granting Ukrainians immediate rights to live and work in EU countries for up to 3 years.
  • UK Homes for Ukraine Scheme: British families sponsored Ukrainian refugees to live in their homes.
  • Humanitarian Aid: Billions in aid from NATO, EU, and individual nations.
  • Global Impact: Disruption to global food and energy markets (wheat, sunflower oil, gas).
