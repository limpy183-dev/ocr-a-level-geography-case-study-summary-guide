Global Migration

Global Migration & Injustices

Inequalities, Conflicts, Human Trafficking & Forced Displacement

1. Key Concepts: The Impacts of Migration

Migration creates complex issues for both the country of origin and the host country. These are categorised into three main areas:

A. Inequalities
  • Brain Drain: Often the better educated migrate, leading to a loss of human resources in the country of origin.
  • Labour Force Loss: Countries of origin lose the youngest, fittest, and most vibrant elements of their workforce, contributing to a downward economic spiral.
  • Demographic Imbalance: Migration causes a redistribution of the population of reproductive age.
    • Origin: Decline in crude birth rates.
    • Destination: Growth in crude birth rates.
  • Remittances: While beneficial, they can increase inequality between families who receive them and those who do not.
B. Conflicts
  • Social Conflict: Can develop between host communities and "newcomers" due to difficulties in integration (e.g., language barriers, cultural differences).
  • Pressure on Services: Concentrated immigrant populations can strain education, health, and housing services in the host country.
  • Border Control: International borders become conflict zones involving border control authorities, traffickers, and illegal migrants.
C. Injustices
  • Human Rights Violations: Migrants are vulnerable to forced labour, exploitation (women/children), and human trafficking.
  • Treatment of Asylum Seekers: Often held in detention centres, denied the right to work, and supported on meagre financial resources for food/sanitation.
  • Refugee Plight: Issues regarding basic needs (shelter, food, water, medicine) and safety. There is also the injustice of potential forced return to dangerous countries of origin.
2. Intraregional Case Study: Turkey (Host & Transit)
Context & Distribution
  • Location: Turkey acts as a major bridge between the Middle East (Syria) and Europe.
  • Camp Distribution: Refugee camps are heavily concentrated in South East Turkey (bordering Syria), in provinces such as Gaziantep, Sanliurfa, and Hatay.
Key Issues & Conflicts
The Greek/Turkish Border:
  • Clashes occur at the border (e.g., Pazarkule/Kastanies).
  • Injustice: Migrants facing freezing conditions (BBC report of 12 deaths).
  • Conflict: Greece blocking thousands of migrants trying to enter; finishing fences to stop surges (e.g., Afghan migrants).
The EU Deal:
  • The EU plans to fund Turkey for border control.
  • Risk: This creates a risk of "forced return" for refugees, violating their rights to safety.
Lesbos (Greek Island):
  • Thousands trying to reach Greek islands from Turkey.
  • Injustice: Poor conditions in camps (e.g., Moria fire) lead to police moving migrants to new, often inadequate, facilities.
3. Case Study: Germany (Destination)
Migrant Groups
  • Turkish Community: Germany has a large, established Turkish population (arrived initially as guest workers).
  • Integration Issues: There are ongoing struggles with integration and identity (e.g., "Turkey's Choice" documentary).
  • Other Groups: Recent surges (2015 migrant crisis) involving Syrian and Afghan refugees have tested cities like Oberhausen.
Synoptic Theme: Contrast

🇩🇪 In Germany

  • Turkish migrants face social challenges regarding integration and cultural identity

🇹🇷 In Turkey

  • Migrants (often Syrian/Afghan) face immediate physical threats, human rights violations at borders, and the threat of deportation
4. Global Trends in Forced Displacement (2011–2020)
Overall Trend: Total forced displacement has nearly doubled from approximately 40 million in 2011 to 80 million by end of 2020.
Forced Migration (IOM Definition): A migratory movement which, although the drivers can be diverse, involves force, compulsion, or coercion.

7. Human Trafficking Trends
Human Trafficking: The recruitment, transportation, or receipt of persons by means of threat or force for the purpose of exploitation (sexual exploitation, forced labour, slavery, or organ removal).
Global Changes (2007–2017)
  • Sexual Exploitation: Generally declined in proportion, though an increase was noted from 2015 onwards due to the inclusion of North American case data.
  • Forced Labour: Has generally increased as identification of such cases has improved.
Regional Differences

Africa & Middle East

  • Higher rates of trafficking for forced labour

Europe & Northern America

  • Higher rates of trafficking for sexual exploitation
